The destination

The island of beans we call a blend is really a recipe. Each component brings something: sweetness from one lot, acidity from another, body from a third. We cup them separately, then in pairs, then together, and only keep the combination that tastes better than any single ingredient would on its own.

We buy green coffee directly from small farms whenever we can, and we visit the ones we can reach. The conversation always starts with the harvest: how the cherries were picked, how long they dried, and what the farmers learned this season. Paying a fair price for careful work is the simplest way we know to keep good coffee on the shelf year after year.

Our espresso blend leans on a Brazilian base for body and chocolate notes, with a washed Ethiopian lot added for a bright floral lift. We tested more than forty ratios before settling on this one. It works well in milk drinks, yet it remains lively enough to enjoy straight, with a syrupy texture and a clean, lingering finish of toasted almond.

Nothing wrong here

Storage is straightforward. Keep your beans in an airtight container away from sunlight, heat and moisture, and grind only what you need for each brew. The refrigerator is a poor choice because it introduces condensation and absorbs odours from other food. A cool cupboard is perfect, and a one way valve bag keeps oxygen out between uses.

Decaffeinated coffee often gets a bad reputation, but modern water processing removes caffeine gently without stripping flavour. We source a Colombian lot decaffeinated with spring water and sugarcane extract, then roast it a touch lighter than usual. Many customers who switch to it in the evening tell us they can no longer tell the difference.
